Not just a number on a dashboard — a one-line diagnosis a marketer can paste into an email.

02 · Explain

01 · PROBLEM

Profound measures AI visibility better than anyone — but customers can't defend, explain, or ship what they see.

Three themes recur across public reviews. Paraphrased, not quoted.

Understand "

"There's a lot of data, but nothing tells me which of these numbers I should care about this week."

Explain "

"My CMO asks why we're losing on certain prompts and I can't give an answer that doesn't sound like a guess."

Ship "

"Even when I know what to do, the fix sits in a doc — it doesn't make it into our sprint."

Who I designed for

A representative user — Maya — built from Profound's case studies (GR0) and the patterns in their reviews.

M

Maya R. — Director of Marketing

Enterprise SaaS · 8-brand portfolio · reports to CMO
  • Wants: a one-page summary she can take into Monday's standup.
  • Hates: being asked "so what?" and having to spend an hour translating dashboards into a doc.
  • Has: a content lead, an SEO contractor, an engineer she shares with product.
  • Measured on: AI-sourced pipeline contribution — not visibility score itself.
Design bet

Profound already knows what is happening and likely knows why. If we surface the diagnosis in plain English, draft the fix, and route it to where work happens — Profound becomes easier to use, easier to defend, and easier to renew.

03 · IMPACT

Four trade-offs, three hypotheses I'd instrument and prove.

Design decisions

Slot in, don't sprawl

Built Action Plan inside Profound's existing ACTION section, next to Opportunities (Beta).

The redesign should look like the next iteration of a roadmap item Profound's team already chose — not a parallel feature. Action Plan is the layer above Opportunities: same data, ranked, AI-explained, ship-ready.

Explicit prioritization

Ranking is shown as impact × effort, not a black-box score.

Marketers escalate decisions. They need to defend ranking choices to a CMO. Showing the two underlying axes — and letting users re-sort — keeps the AI in a thinking-partner role, not an oracle. Trust compounds faster.

Drafts over briefs

Each recipe ships an editable draft, not a recommendation doc.

"Improve your comparison content" isn't actionable; a 1,200-word draft of acme.com/vs/vendora with FAQ schema attached is. This converts Profound's data into commits, not memos — and creates a clean expansion vector: each recipe is a candidate for a paid execution add-on.

Meet work where it lives

One-click handoff to Linear, Jira, GitHub, Slack, or Email.

Marketing teams don't run their sprints inside SEO tools. Profound becomes the planning layer; the customer's existing stack stays the execution layer. This preserves the data moat without trying to replace tools customers already love.

Business hypotheses

What I'd want to instrument if I were on the team shipping this. Not promises.

↓ 60%

Time to first action

From "logged in" to "shipped a fix" — the metric most correlated with retention in tools-of-record.

↑ NRR

Expansion vector

Each recipe is a candidate for a paid execution add-on — Profound writes the page, Profound seeds the Reddit reply.

→ Moat

Defensibility

Pure measurement is becoming a commodity. Owning the action layer is the durable position.
